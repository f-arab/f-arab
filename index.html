About

I am a postdoctoral scholar in the Brain Networks Lab (Raj Lab) at the University of California San Francisco. My path into computational neuroscience has not been a straight line, and I am grateful for that. I earned my bachelor's degree in Electrical Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology in Iran, with a double major in Biomedical Engineering, and went on to complete a master's degree in Electrical Engineering, Communication Systems, also at Amirkabir University of Technology. I then worked as a signal processing engineer. Even in those early years, I was fascinated by the idea that the brain could be understood as a dynamical system, and that fascination never left me.

Halfway through my PhD, I decided to officially switch to the field I had always been drawn to: computational neuroscience. Coming from an engineering-heavy background, I committed myself to learning the biological side of the brain in depth, and neuroscience remains the thing I enjoy learning about every single day. During the second half of my PhD, working with Professor Erfan Nozari at UC Riverside, I built my foundation in seeing the brain as a dynamical system and a complex network, with a focus on whole-brain causal discovery and a deeper understanding of the brain through the lens of graph theory.

Beginning my postdoc in the Raj Lab, working with Professor Ashish Raj, opened a new perspective for me, one centered on modeling the brain through structure-function (SC-FC) relationships and network diffusion, and UCSF has given me the exposure and the opportunity to study human brain dynamics in the context of brain disease. My main project models the progression of tau pathology in Alzheimer's disease using network diffusion models and multimodal imaging such as DTI and fMRI, and alongside it I lead and contribute to several projects that continue my long-standing passion for understanding the brain as a dynamic system, often through functional MRI (fMRI). I am grateful for this nonlinear research path: it taught me to look at every problem I am given from more than one perspective, and it keeps every day a learning day. I plan to keep it that way.
